# 🛡️ How I Found 300+ Web3 Users’ 2FA Secrets Using Just One Recon Command

## Hey, this is zack0x01 👋

I’m a bug bounty hunter focused on Web3 apps, and this write-up is for the real ones in the game — the researchers.

In this post, I’ll walk you through how I discovered a super simple but **high-impact bug** on a Web3 platform (part of a private HackerOne program) that exposed:

- Full names
- Emails
- And raw **TOTP secrets** of **300+ users**

With just one command and a bit of curiosity, I had everything needed to bypass 2FA and fully compromise accounts — no exploits, no bruteforcing, just **poor implementation and a third-party mistake**.

## 🔍 Recon Starts Everything

While reviewing 2FA setup, I noticed something sketchy in the frontend logic:

`<img src="https://genQrcode.com/{base64}">`
The QR code used to enroll in Google Authenticator was being pulled from an external domain — `genQrcode.com`. The payload? Just a Base64 string.

This smelled like a lazy shortcut. I dumped the domain through **Waymore** to confirm.

## 🧪 The Command

`waymore -i genQrcode.com -o exposed_qrcodes.txt`
What I got back was wild — **hundreds** of publicly indexed paths, all looking like QR codes.

I decoded a few Base64 strings from the URLs manually:

`echo "Sm9obiBEb2U7am9obi5kb2VAZXhhbXBsZS5jb207SkJTV1kzRFBFSFBLM1BY" | base64 -d`
Output:

`John Doe;john.doe@example.com;JBSWY3DPEHPK3PXP`
That’s:

- 👤 `Full Name`
- 📧 `Email`
- 🔐 `TOTP Secret Key` (used for 2FA in Google Authenticator)

And I wasn’t looking at a single user — this affected **hundreds**, live.

## 🎯 Real-World Exploitation Scenario

The app used **TOTP-based 2FA**, and this QR was how users enrolled. So an attacker with this info could:

1. Load the TOTP secret into any authenticator app
2. Phish or reuse credentials
3. Enter valid 6-digit 2FA codes and bypass login protection
4. Fully take over user accounts

On a Web3 platform, this can mean access to **wallets, private keys, linked exchanges**, and more.

## 🧨 Why This Bug Was Dangerous

Mistake Result QR generated by 3rd party Data lives outside your control Base64 encoding used No encryption at all No auth / expiry / binding Anyone can access and reuse Indexed by crawlers Recon tools find it instantly

This was a full-on **2FA bypass chain** — but with zero exploitation required. Just decoding.

## 💬 Final Thoughts :

This bug wasn’t advanced — no need for fuzzers, emulators, or exploits.

It was **pure recon + logic + common sense**.

So yeah, next time you see a weird external QR or a random Base64 string in the wild:

👉 **Decode it.**

👉 **Check who’s hosting it.**

👉 **Assume nothing is protected unless proven.**

**The lesson here?**

*Third-party dependencies are often the weakest link — and when they’re in your auth flow, it’s game over.*


Keep hunting, keep digging, and don’t sleep on the simple stuff.
