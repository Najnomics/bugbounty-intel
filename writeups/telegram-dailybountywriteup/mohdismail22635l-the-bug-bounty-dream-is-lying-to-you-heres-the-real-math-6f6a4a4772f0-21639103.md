“I’ll make lakhs from bug bounty.” That’s the dream thousands chase the moment they step into cybersecurity. Twitter threads show six-figure payouts. YouTube thumbnails scream “I made ₹10 lakh in 3 months.” Everyone wants in.

Six months later, most of those same people either burn out quietly — or start blaming the entire field for “not working.”

Here’s the truth nobody puts in a thumbnail: bug bounty isn’t a lottery you win by trying harder. It’s a skill game with brutal math behind it. And almost nobody talks about what that math actually looks like.

**The Hype vs. The Reality**

Every platform — HackerOne, Bugcrowd, Synack — has a public leaderboard. And that leaderboard is the problem. It only shows you the top 1%: researchers who find zero-days, work alongside full-time security teams, and have fundamentals so deep they can spot a flaw in seconds, not hours.

What it doesn’t show you is the other 99% — people submitting report after report, getting back the same three words: “duplicate,” “informational,” or “not applicable.” That’s not bad luck. That’s usually a fundamentals gap. Most beginners run a tool, see red text, and submit — without ever asking why that vulnerability exists in the first place.

**The Numbers Nobody Shares**

This is the part that actually matters, and it rarely gets said out loud:

1.Even when you find a real, valid bug, it can still get closed as “informational” — zero payout, zero explanation beyond a template message.

2.Small companies running their own private programs usually have tiny security budgets. Sometimes there’s no budget for payouts at all — just a thank-you and a spot on their “Hall of Fame” page.

3.Timing matters more than skill sometimes. You can find a legitimate issue and still get nothing because someone reported the exact same thing an hour before you did.

4.Platforms triage thousands of reports a day. Yours is one more email in a queue, not a guaranteed payday.

Nobody puts this in a “How I Made ₹5 Lakh” video, because it doesn’t sell the dream.

**A Real, Dramatic Example**

Let’s actually do the math, because numbers hit harder than warnings.

One beginner grinded bug bounty for six straight months. Late nights after college, burnout, rejection emails piling up in the inbox, the constant mental loop of “maybe tomorrow I’ll find something big.” Total earnings at the end of those six months: ₹1 to ₹1.5 lakh.

Break that down and it’s roughly ₹20,000–25,000 a month — and that’s before accounting for the hours that went completely unpaid, the reports that got closed, and the burnout that came with it.

Now compare that to the alternative. In that same six-month window, he could have taken a structured path instead: a SOC analyst role, a pentesting internship, or freelance security consulting work. Any of those would’ve given him stable income every single month, real mentorship from people who’d already made the mistakes he was making blindly, and — just as important — a resume line that actually means something to a hiring manager.

Instead, those six months went almost entirely into chasing uncertainty. Not because he wasn’t capable. Because the order was wrong.

**Why the Order Matters**

Here’s the pattern that separates people who eventually succeed at bug bounty from people who quit and blame the industry:

The ones who make it treat bug bounty as something you add on top of a solid base — networking concepts, how the web actually works under the hood, OWASP fundamentals, how authentication and sessions behave. They understand why a bug exists before they ever go looking for one.

The ones who burn out treat bug bounty as the starting point itself — install a tool, scan a target, hope for green text, repeat. When nothing comes, frustration turns into “this industry is a scam,” when really, the foundation was never built.

**When Bug Bounty Actually Works**

1.Your fundamentals are genuinely solid — not just watched-a-video solid, but can-explain-it-to-someone-else solid

2.You understand why a vulnerability exists, not just what a tool’s output told you

3.You treat it as a long-term skill you build alongside a job or income source — not a replacement for one from day one

4.You’re prepared for a 95%+ rejection rate and don’t take it personally

**Final Thought**

Bug bounty isn’t fake, and it isn’t a scam. People genuinely do make serious money from it — but almost always after years of fundamentals, not instead of them.

Fundamentals first. Bounty second. Flip that order, and the only thing guaranteed is frustration, not income.

So before you open another scanning tool tonight, ask yourself one honest question: do I actually understand how this works — or am I just hoping for green text?
