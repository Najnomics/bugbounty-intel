An ongoing hacking spree has claimed another three victims in the past 24 hours, with a total of over $35 million lost.

Verus bridge has lost over $7.5 million, just two months after being hit by a similar hack which claimed $11 million. Proceeds of the May exploit were partially returned following a 25% bounty offer.

Now, AFX is [offering](https://etherscan.io/tx/0xaaa471bbe2c187ef674338b7a4cab242c6723a436fb9637b2928152f7f14f5c9) a 30% bounty in response to losing **$24 million USDC** from its Arbitrum bridge.

In an increasingly [bleak landscape](https://blockthreat.com/the-breaking-point-of-ethical-security-research/) for legitimate security researchers, such generous offers are, at best, [insulting](https://x.com/only01Essential/status/2058114455444021566), and, at worst, may even tempt those with such skills to the dark side.

## Verus bridge hacked again

Following the return of 75% of the funds lost in May’s hack, the assets were sent from the [recovery address](https://etherscan.io/address/0xf9ab28cb7b72b518e6a351fbdabe69362cbc1a74) back into the Verus bridge just 14 days ago.

In the early hours of Thursday, a wide range of assets (tBTC, ETH, USDC, scrvUSD, MKR, USDT and EURC) totaling $7.5 million were withdrawn from the bridge in a single [transaction](https://etherscan.io/tx/0xa1f1e65c1cea4dba4ae439cd4dcdba6cc2dbda0ed1228e61f29ae9c9324eb099).

According to blockchain auditor SlowMist, both exploits [share](https://x.com/SlowMist_Team/status/2080192064478724299) a root cause of “flawed cross-chain import validation,” though with slightly different attack vectors.

This time it looks much less likely that Verus will see the money again, however. The [attacker](https://etherscan.io/txs?a=0xcfd0a20703cd11e0b9f665e1c3f1ef989c142d54&f=2&ps=50) has since deposited a total of **3,916 ETH (over $6.6 million)** to Tornado Cash.

## AFX’s USDC bridge drained of $24M

Late on Wednesday, AFX’s USDC custody bridge on Arbitrum was [drained](https://arbiscan.io/tx/0x50d0b3ec6c3f5fce0f10abf81540bbb508f421494aa2b3480c4a264b0436547b) of over $24 million. Security firm BlockSec [believes](https://x.com/Phalcon_xyz/status/2080139684790092275) this to be a “malicious use of authorized validator keys,” which were used to sign “the bridge’s 5-of-7 validator quorum.”

Responding to the incident, AFX [stated](https://x.com/AFX_XYZ/status/2080126901205770734) it had suspended bridge operations and is “investigating the root cause.”

It reassured users that its “AFX trading infrastructure, mainnet, and the Arbitrum network itself have not been compromised.”

AFX has also [offered](https://arbiscan.io/tx/0x0807d0bfc4909636ed7d17cccb7bfe35128da0e5f6c07d836797f05b41d46195) a 30% bounty, worth $7.2 million, for the return of the remaining funds, “as a white hat bounty.” Security expert Taylor Monahan, again, [questions](https://x.com/tayvano_/status/2080166966694928480) the wisdom of such a move.

*Read more: More oracle exploits as Ostium loses over $20M*

Another audit firm, Peckshield, [notes](https://x.com/PeckShieldAlert/status/2080088731558801909) that the exploiter has since swapped the funds for over 12,000 ETH, worth approximately $24 million. Funds remain in the attacker’s [address](https://etherscan.io/address/0x627654b2782bfc57580ecd11d40869b350b6ebac) on Ethereum.

Since the beginning of [last week](https://protos.com/across-allbridge-teleswap-lost-5-7m-to-bridge-hacks-in-past-week/), bridge exploits have claimed **at least $40 million**.

## BSquared staking contract emptied

Finally, BTC-for-AI-agents project BSquared was hacked for almost $4 million due to “unauthorized access to the staking contract’s upgrade authority.”

The team has [promised](https://x.com/BSquaredNetwork/status/2080077286238454109) affected B2 stakers will be fully compensated, and is [offering](https://bscscan.com/tx/0x29d3031a1d331cffdb1607153be9ee20adcad8c55d51bc32fc96c6f358cf5ea2) the standard 10% bounty.

*Read more: Supra patched oracle on 11 other chains before $9M Hedera exploit*

The contract was drained of $3.86 million worth of B2 tokens on BNB Chain. According to blockchain investigator Specter, which [flagged](https://x.com/SpecterAnalyst/status/2079997330393010338) the theft, the tokens were swapped to WBNB, bridged to Ethereum and moved to privacy protocol Zcash.

They [claim](https://x.com/SpecterAnalyst/status/2080002447389413632) that the privileged role has been active for over a year, which may point to an inside job.
