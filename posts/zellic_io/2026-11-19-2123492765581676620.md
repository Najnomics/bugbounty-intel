## Analysis

**Crypto / low-level research (Zellic, part 2 of their x86 formal-verification project):** they (tried to) formally verify the glibc `memrchr` variants, hit bugs that blocked proving the intended statement, and showed via formal verification that **those bugs are the only bugs**.
**Full writeup saved:** [Zellic — x86 formal verification: formally verifying glibc memrchr](../../writeups/zellic_io/x86-formal-verification-memrchr.md).
**Harness takeaway:** for hand-written SIMD/assembly string routines (memchr/memrchr/strlen families), formal verification both finds correctness bugs and *bounds* them (proves absence beyond the found set). For a harness this is a template for high-assurance review of small, security-critical primitives (crypto constant-time code, parsers, allocators): spec the intended postcondition, symbolically execute the asm, and treat every proof failure as either a bug or a missing precondition.
