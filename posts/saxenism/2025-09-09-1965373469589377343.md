> Centralisation Risks. 
> 
> When thinking strictly from a web3 security perspective, "centralisation risks" means that an admin address can decide to blow their users' funds from the TVL on cocaine and hookers.
> 
> Since that is not very likely to happen, everyone becomes "deaf" to this risk in protocols.
> 
> But, when thinking about it from a web2 perspective, this attack vector suddenly becomes much stronger and quite plausible.
> 
> You realise that not only is it about the intention of the admin that can lead to a protocol rug/hack. But the security of the ENTIRE off-chain component decides how real the centralisation threat really is.
> 
> Every critical service running on the default port with a chatty banner and shit firewall increases the likelihood that an attacker infiltrates the off-chain infra and gains privileged access to on-chain smart contract actions.
> 
> Therefore, for protocols with generous amounts of centralisation, the security of the entire stack becomes EVEN more important.
