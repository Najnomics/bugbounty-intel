> Agreed, it's builder-focused, that's the audience. And users may tolerate downtime if the benefits are clear. My point isn't about going for high-nine SLAs.
> 
> The difference is architectural: when a normal service fails, you can often degrade gracefully. When a TEE providing privacy or confidentiality fails, graceful degradation often means leaking exactly what you were protecting.
> 
> This isn’t a 4-nines problem; it’s a design problem. Protocols need to be designed so that temporary unavailability doesn’t force unsafe fallbacks or leave them in irrecoverable states.
> 
> It’s also worth expanding the threat model beyond “TEE provider has an outage” to “TEE provider is adversarial and makes instances unavailable at the worst possible moment.” Availability failures become DoS vectors when the attacker controls the host.
