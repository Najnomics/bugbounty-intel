> You can absolutely get started with building on TEEs with a few clicks (AWS Nitro enclaves or similar confidential compute solutions).
> 
> But as you really start to think about the end-to-end architecture, you realise that you are working on quite a bunch of trust assumptions.
> 
> And EACH of these trust assumptions take a huge effort to be written away from your threat model.
> 
> Reproducible builds are one of those massive trust assumptions that everyone knows and gets but still everyone just makes peace with it, b/c it is just THAT HARD to actually mitigate for it.
> 
> This issue can be boiled down to: you having to blindly trust that the binary/image you are working with is indeed the correct binary, obtained by the correct compilation of a given source code. 
> 
> It is such a brutally hard problem that only humongous corporations such as Microsoft, Google, etc... even have some mitigations against it.
> 
> Post the release of the TEE Security Handbook, @weijie_eth reached out, informing me about StageX, their (reproducible build + verifiable bootstrapping pipeline) solution, that ensures application binaries can be reproduced, agreed upon and certified by multiple parties.
> 
> I just read their "Verifiable Foundations" blog and I was completely fucking blown away by the excellent work done by @turnkeyhq on mitigating this massive trust assumption for the entire web3 industry.
> 
> StageX by @turnkeyhq is not only built on accessible components, they intentionally used a stack that made it possible for external researchers to reproduce their model.
> + They took something that would prolly take ~$100M and made it doable by a small team
> + They have open-sourced a significant component of their architecture, making them WAY more open that Google, Meta, AWS, etc.
> + Their stack is extremely simple/approachable compared to the bigwigs in the space
> + They tackled and actively talked about "Verifiable Bootstrapping" which is astonishingly missing from all conversations around reproducible builds, attestations or supply-chain metadata
> + Most importantly, StageX is web3-native and web3-first. This is one of the very FEW pieces of serious work coming out of our industry.
> 
> I was completely fucking blown away reading their work and as they themselves say in the blog, "The fact that StageX works is a miracle...", it truly felt magical to see how the entire system comes together and it just... works.
> 
> Massive massive shoutout to @turnkeyhq for their exceptional work.
> 
> PS: Thanks @arnaudbrousseau for prompting me to look deeper into Turnkey XD
> PPS: No prizes for guessing the next deep-dive section in the TEE Security Handbook. (It'll be a deeper dive into trust assumptions and their mitigations... starting from the reproducible build issue)
