> I think this depends heavily on how TEEs are used.
> 
> If TEEs are just protecting keys or small coprocessor logic inside an otherwise normal validator, then yes. This mostly reduces to a standard liveness problem handled by consensus.
> 
> But in TEE-heavy designs, where correctness depends on private enclave execution, other validators often can't independently re-execute or audit that logic. That shifts trust from protocol-level verification to h/w trust and attestation, which has centralisation and failure-mode implications.
> 
> Availability then isn't just limited to "some validators are offline", but also "opaque components are unavailable or censored in correlated ways"... especially under shared cloud or h/w dependencies.
> 
> There's also a more subtle bind here. If the TEE is providing confidentiality or privacy guarantees, you often can't safely fall back to non-TEE execution when it goes down, WITHOUT breaking the very property the TEE exists to provide. That makes availability fundamentally different from “just restart or migrate the validator.” 
> 
> THINK: If your TEE protects private orderflow, secrets, keys, or hidden state -> And the TEE goes down -> You usually cannot safely fall back to non-TEE execution -> Because doing so leaks or corrupts exactly what you were protecting
> 
> m-of-n style MPC setups help, but they still fail if enough enclaves are simultaneously paused, switched or unreachable.
