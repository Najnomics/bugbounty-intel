> Giving LLMs permissions outside a completely contained sandbox was always a bad idea -- and will keep being a bad idea.
>
> If you wan to let those things act without asking for confirmation all the time (and the default  should  be not wanting it) you need to start by defining exactly what it can be allowed to do, in the most restrictive way possible. Ideally not even a command line -- it can call a few scripts that do very limited things, and you heavily scrutinize those scripts.
>
> Treat LLMs as adversaries bc they will try to replicate whatever behavior they learned from the internet and RLHF and who knows what sort of thing that means. 
>
> Sysadmins would never give any new human hire the sort of permissions to do that. But apparently LLMs are now God so we need to trust them with everything.
